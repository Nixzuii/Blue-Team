# Introduction to SOC.

**SOC** or **Security Operation Center** is the centralized command hub where an organization's cybersecurity defense is planned, executed, and refined. It's not just a room full of monitors — it's a combination of people, processes, and technology working 24/7 to protect digital assets. The SOC's mission can be summed up as: monitor, detect, analyze, and respond to threats in real time, while continuously improving the organization's security posture. Unlike ad-hoc or "reactive only" security, a mature SOC is proactive — it hunts for threats, identifies weaknesses before attackers exploit them, and refines defenses based on every incident. ### 1. Identify

- Recognize an alert as potentially malicious.
- Open an incident ticket and begin tracking.
- Use tools like SIEM, XDR, and threat intelligence feeds to spot anomalies.

### 2. Investigate

- Determine the **root cause** and **scope** of the incident.
- Correlate data across systems (endpoints, network, cloud, logs).
- Understand attacker TTPs (tactics, techniques, and procedures).

### 3. Mitigate

- Recommend and execute actions to **isolate** and **remove** the threat.
- Examples: shutting down compromised endpoints, suspending accounts, quarantining infected files, blocking malicious traffic.

### 4. Continuous Improvement

- After every incident, refine detection rules, update playbooks, and patch gaps.
- The SOC gets smarter with each event.
